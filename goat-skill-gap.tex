\title{Statistical Rules of Greatness in Team Sports\\
\large Historical careers, peer-relative output, and the limits of a common rule}
\author{}
\date{}


\begin{document}
\maketitle
\vspace{-1.5em}
\begin{abstract}
We search for robust statistical rules that could explain greatest-of-all-time (GOAT) player status in team sports.
Historical career populations, contribution measures, documented all-time selections, and four million soccer events provide comparisons across generations; recent-season encounter models test proposed count-based rules.
Gretzky's lead over the next career points total is 48.72 percent, but his lead over the next career points-per-game rate is 2.03 percent; Jordan's career scoring-rate lead is 0.19 percent.
Rate comparisons require at least 500 regular-season games.
Ruth leads combined batting-and-pitching WAR with 182.6, despite ranking third in home runs.
Dominance therefore depends on the measure and comparison population.
None of four primary recent-season output associations survives multiple-testing correction.
A count-based superiority threshold admits 43--56 percent of eligible athletes, while two apparently uncorrelated competition ratios share an exact mathematical identity.
These results do not establish a common rule of greatness.
They expose problems of selectivity, observation period, and arithmetic coupling, but do not demonstrate that meaningful common patterns are absent.
The measures capture selected contributions and encounters; published elite selections supply recognition outcomes but do not define a common GOAT ranking.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and scope}

Can a player's superiority over peers, the size of the opposing population, or team size explain GOAT status across team sports?
We compare historical career totals and rates, then examine count-based rules against observed encounters and role-matched output rates.
A useful rule would need to distinguish recognised exceptional players from comparison athletes and remain informative across competition settings.
Historical league records supply comparison athletes across generations.
Published league selections provide an external recognition outcome, although selectors themselves consider performance statistics.
Their definitions differ, and recent-season encounter tests cannot establish all-time greatness.

Output is also task-specific.
Hitting, goalscoring, and hockey points measure different contributions.
Their gaps can be expressed relative to comparable peers, but this does not make them complete measures of skill or give every teammate an equal share of success.

\section{Data and measures}

\subsection{Historical career populations}

A checksummed archive contains 3.88 GB in 3,211 downloaded source files, with reproducible collectors and an audited player database.
File volume is not an independent sample size.
The main historical populations are:

Baseball data come directly from the \src{https://sabr.org/lahman-database/}{SABR/Sean Lahman database}, including recorded Negro League statistics.

NHL data come from the \src{https://www.nhl.com/stats/}{official statistics API}, with every five-season response checked for complete retrieval.
NBA multi-team totals are retained once and reconcile exactly to team-stint games and points.
Direct \src{https://www.baseball-reference.com/about/war_explained.shtml}{Baseball Reference WAR exports} add 125,717 batting/value and 56,882 pitching rows through 2025, combining recorded batting, fielding, baserunning, position, pitching, and context adjustments.
Career and best-seven-season totals remain separate; the seven peak seasons need not be consecutive.
NBA Win Shares and VORP are retained with explicit historical coverage, rather than treating missing early values as zero.
WAR is a model-dependent contribution estimate, not a direct measurement of complete skill.

The \src{https://github.com/nflverse/nflverse-data/releases/tag/stats_player}{nflverse records} start in 1999.
Direct NFL.com downloads add 468 passing tables and 3,564 recorded player-seasons from 1950--2025; all displayed totals reconcile to their season rows, but this does not establish complete careers.
For example, NFL.com's Baugh table contains only 2,386 passing yards, omitting 1937--1949.
Thirty-five \src{https://www.profootballhof.com/hall-of-famers/positions}{Hall of Fame quarterback profiles} add 33 passing tables and 476 year rows, including Baugh's full 21,886 yards and separate Graham totals of 13,499 NFL yards and 23,584 NFL-plus-AAFC yards.
Pre-1932 measures and inconsistent totals remain flagged; these selected profiles are not a historical player census.
The nflverse Brady total differs from NFL.com's record by two yards and two attempts; sources are not silently spliced.
Postseason and regular-season records remain separate.

\textbf{Soccer performance and denominators.}
\src{https://github.com/hudl/open-data}{StatsBomb Open Data} contributes 4,059,733 events in 1,130 selected matches, producing 31,790 player-match records for 4,212 identities.
The sample includes 116 women's World Cup matches and the complete 380-match La Liga 2015/16 season.
Goals, nonpenalty goals, xG, assists, shots, passes, and elapsed playing seconds replace bench listings as performance measures.
Playing time uses lineups, substitutions, dismissals, on/off events, and period ends, including added and extra time; shootouts are excluded.
All match scores reconcile. Source clock anomalies remain documented, and exposure is bounded by recorded period ends.

Messi's La Liga records contain 519 appearances and 472 goals, versus \src{https://www.fcbarcelona.com/en/card/2214377/leo-messi}{Barcelona's 520 and 474}, documenting an unresolved coverage gap.
The \src{https://www.rsssf.org/miscellaneous/century.html}{RSSSF international scoring list} supplies dated career goals and caps for 370 men with at least 30 goals, including Messi, Ronaldo, Pel\'e, and Maradona.
This is a selected scoring population, not complete club careers or equivalent opposition.
Incomplete international goal-event rows are not used as career totals.

\textbf{Recognition and comparison populations.}
Official \src{https://www.nba.com/news/nba-75th-anniversary-team-announced}{NBA75}, \src{https://www.nhl.com/news/100-greatest-nhl-players-complete-list-286199184}{NHL100}, and \src{https://www.nfl.com/news/nfl-100-all-time-team-quarterbacks-announced-0ap3000001091949}{NFL100 quarterback} lists provide 76, 100, and 10 selections, respectively.
NBA75 comparison features stop in 2020/21 and NHL100 features in 2015/16, before their selection dates, preventing later achievements from explaining earlier recognition.
The panels contain 4,586 NBA/BAA players, 6,673 NHL skaters, and 758 goalies; all NBA and NHL selections match verified identities and published roles.
ABA and WHA contributions are excluded from these league panels.
These are documented elite selections, not 186 GOAT labels or a universal ranking; no cross-sport recognition model is fitted here.
Historical missingness, incomplete Negro League records, and limited women's and other-sport coverage constrain generalisation.

\textbf{Sources and encounter definition.}
\src{https://www.retrosheet.org/downloads/plays.html}{Retrosheet parsed plays} identify batters and active defenders.
The count includes completed PA and nonterminal events with recorded pitches; missing pitch histories make it an observed lower bound.
\src{https://github.com/hudl/open-data}{StatsBomb Open Data} supplies soccer lineups, substitutions, dismissals, events, and expected goals.
The \src{https://api.nhle.com/stats/rest/en/shiftcharts?cayenneExp=gameId=2023020001}{NHL shift records} and \src{https://api-web.nhle.com/v1/gamecenter/2023020001/boxscore}{box scores} identify active hockey players.
Soccer and hockey encounters require positive shared playing time.
Bench membership and repeated appearances do not create additional distinct opponents.
These are opposing-unit encounters, not necessarily direct physical duels.
Stable identities prevent a transferred opponent from being counted twice.

The source analysis reports reconciled baseball outcomes and soccer scores, and agreement between reconstructed and exported opponent sets.
Nine NHL games have shift-versus-boxscore time discrepancies exceeding one second, with a maximum player discrepancy of 61 seconds.
Their exclusion is a quality sensitivity below.
The historical archive supplies new career and soccer-event tables, but not the original MLB, EPL, WSL, and NHL encounter-model tables.
The encounter regressions below are reported source results, rather than independently rerun fits.

\subsection{Encounter counts and output superiority}

Let $L$ denote competition teams, $K_i$ opposing clubs faced, and $N_i$ distinct opposing individuals faced during the specified period.
The computed opposing-team size is $m_i^{\rm eff}=N_i/K_i$.
It measures the distinct-person union per opposing club, rather than simultaneous lineup size, independent opposition, or causal impact share.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Cohort & Mean $N$ per game & Median period $N$ & Period $N$ range & Median $m^{\rm eff}$ \\
\midrule
MLB 2023 & 11.006 & 591 & 374--716 & 21.89 \\
MLB 2024 & 10.977 & 592 & 367--720 & 21.76 \\
EPL 2015/16 & 13.193 & 284 & 110--358 & 16.00 \\
WSL 2023/24 & 13.970 & 192 & 117--221 & 17.55 \\
NHL sample & 19.000 & 114 & 57--134 & 19.00 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Game means use all observed batter-games, active soccer player-games including goalkeepers, and NHL skater-games.
Period summaries use eligible output cohorts.
The effective sizes are medians of individual ratios, not ratios of separate medians.
An athlete can face about eleven defenders in one baseball game but hundreds over a season.
Consequently, an inverse-count threshold changes with the period even when underlying ability does not.
For example, Judge and Ohtani face observed 2024 unions of 676 and 681 defenders, giving $1/N$ thresholds of about 0.148 and 0.147 percent.
These numbers measure exposure arithmetic, not percentage advantages in complete skill.

For output $y_i$ and opportunity $e_i$, define a role-matched, opportunity-weighted benchmark and relative gap:
\begin{equation}
q_i=\frac{y_i}{e_i},\qquad
\mu_{sr}=\frac{\sum_{j\in(s,r)}y_j}{\sum_{j\in(s,r)}e_j},\qquad
\delta_i=\frac{q_i-\mu_{sr}}{\mu_{sr}}.
\label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
The benchmark includes all positive-opportunity athletes in the cohort and broad role, including teammates, low-opportunity players, and the focal athlete.
It is a descriptive peer reference, not faced-opponent strength.
Outputs are hits per completed PA, nonpenalty goals per elapsed minute, and points per ice minute.
Soccer roles are defender, midfielder, and forward; hockey roles are defender and forward.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Career dominance depends on the statistic}

The downloaded records reproduce Gretzky's 2,857 NHL regular-season points in 1,487 games and Jordan's 32,292 NBA points in 1,072 games.
Gretzky exceeds Jagr's 1,921 career points by 48.72 percent.
Among skaters with at least 500 games, his 1.9213 points per game exceeds Lemieux's 1.8831 by only 2.03 percent.
Among NBA/BAA players meeting the same game threshold, Jordan's 30.1231 points per game exceeds Chamberlain's 30.0660 by 0.19 percent.
Jordan ranks fifth in accumulated points through 2024/25; LeBron leads with 42,184.
In AL/NL baseball, Ruth's 714 home runs trail Bonds's 762 and Aaron's 755.
However, combined batting/value and pitching WAR puts Ruth first at 182.62, including 20.36 pitching WAR, ahead of Walter Johnson's 168.35.
A home-run comparison omits contributions that materially alter the assessment.

These examples reject treating a lead in one raw career statistic as a uniform percentage advantage in skill.
They are descriptive comparisons, not significance tests: rates are not adjusted for era, pace, role, or league strength, and the 500-game filter defines a comparison population rather than greatness.
In the complete La Liga 2015/16 sample, Su\'arez, Ronaldo, and Messi score 40, 35, and 26 goals, respectively; their nonpenalty goals per 90 elapsed minutes are 1.017, 0.790, and 0.731.
A career reputation need not identify the leader in an individual season.
Neither these famous examples nor the published elite lists define a common ordinal GOAT outcome.

\subsection{Output associations do not survive correction}

Within each sport, the fitted model is
\begin{equation}
\delta_i=\alpha+\beta\left(\frac{100}{N_i}\right)+\gamma^\top Z_i+\varepsilon_i.
\label{eq:regression}
\end{equation}
Controls include log opportunity, game count, opposing-club count, broad role where applicable, and year for pooled MLB.
Continuous controls are standardised.
Standard errors use corrected own-club cluster covariance with cluster-count-minus-one $t$ degrees of freedom; cluster counts are 30, 20, 12, and 32.
Shared opponents and estimated cohort benchmarks may create additional dependence.
The 12-club WSL inference is particularly approximate.
The covariance follows \src{https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/generated/statsmodels.stats.sandwich_covariance.cov_cluster_2groups.html}{cluster-sandwich methods}; \src{https://cameron.econ.ucdavis.edu/research/Cameron_Miller_JHR_2015_February.pdf}{cluster-inference guidance} describes the dependence and small-sample limitations.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Model & $n$ & $\widehat\beta$ & Nominal 95\% interval & Nominal $p$ & Holm $p$ \\
\midrule
MLB, two seasons & 579 & 1.977 & $[0.311,3.642]$ & 0.022 & 0.087 \\
EPL 2015/16 & 305 & -0.342 & $[-7.197,6.514]$ & 0.918 & 0.918 \\
WSL 2023/24 & 145 & 7.371 & $[-5.649,20.390]$ & 0.239 & 0.477 \\
NHL sample & 525 & 2.236 & $[-0.374,4.845]$ & 0.090 & 0.271 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

None of the four primary tests meets the Holm-adjusted 0.05 criterion.
MLB has the strongest nominal association, but its significance depends on opportunity eligibility: $p=0.006$ at 150 PA and $p=0.145$ at 502 PA, versus $p=0.022$ at the primary 300 PA cutoff.
Two-way club/player clustering gives nominal $p=0.021$ for the primary MLB sample.
For NHL, removing all nine flagged games leaves 491 eligible skaters and reverses the slope to $-0.948$ ($p=0.420$).
EPL club-deletion slopes range from $-2.655$ to $1.847$, also changing sign.
These sensitivities prevent treating a favourable specification as a robust finding.

The models test adjusted output associations with an inverse count.
They do not establish an inverse-count law of skill, a causal effect of opposition, or a relationship with independently measured GOAT status.

\subsection{The superiority threshold is weakly selective}

The candidate condition $\delta_i>1/N_i$ requires output superiority greater than the inverse distinct-opponent count.
It admits a substantial fraction of each cohort:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
Cohort & Pass $\delta>1/N$ & Fraction passing & Pass $\delta>1/L$ \\
\midrule
MLB 2023 & 163/293 & 55.6\% & 45.7\% \\
MLB 2024 & 153/286 & 53.5\% & 45.5\% \\
EPL 2015/16 & 139/305 & 45.6\% & 43.0\% \\
WSL 2023/24 & 65/145 & 44.8\% & 37.9\% \\
NHL sample & 227/525 & 43.2\% & 42.5\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

At large $N$, the threshold approaches a test of being above the role mean.
Passing therefore supplies little evidence of exceptional status by itself.
The fractions are not false-positive rates: no independent GOAT labels were assigned.
The alternative team-count condition also admits 38--46 percent, rather than selecting a narrow exceptional group.

A five-case comparison of Ruth, Gretzky, Burrow, Messi, and Jordan, using selected output gaps and an equal-lineup-share assumption, gives nominal $p=0.139$ and permutation $p=0.183$.
The selected names and incompatible output measures do not support a general rule.

\subsection{Near-zero aggregate correlation conceals exact coupling}

Consider two competition ratios,
\begin{equation}
A_i=\frac{1}{N_i+1},\qquad
B_i=\frac{m_i^{\rm eff}}{L}=\frac{N_i}{K_iL}.
\label{eq:ratios}
\end{equation}
The five eligible-cohort medians are:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
Cohort & Median $A$ & Median $B$ \\
\midrule
MLB 2023 & 0.001689 & 0.7296 \\
MLB 2024 & 0.001686 & 0.7253 \\
EPL 2015/16 & 0.003509 & 0.8000 \\
WSL 2023/24 & 0.005181 & 1.4621 \\
NHL sample & 0.008696 & 0.5938 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

With cohorts equally weighted, Pearson $r=0.0253$ ($p=0.968$); exhaustive permutation of all 120 assignments gives $p=0.925$.
Using instantaneous lineup size divided by $L$ instead gives $r=-0.0109$ and permutation $p=1.000$.
Neither calculation establishes practical independence.
The MLB years repeat one competition, and the NHL period is much shorter than the other periods, so these probabilities are descriptive diagnostics rather than validated population inference.

More fundamentally,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{B_i=\frac{1/A_i-1}{K_iL}.}
\label{eq:coupling}
\end{equation}
At fixed $K,L$, $B$ decreases deterministically with $A$; their rank correlation is $-1$ whenever $N$ varies.
Variation in $K,L$ can conceal the relationship when groups are pooled.
For example, the reported within-cohort rank correlations are $-0.911$ in EPL and $-0.945$ in WSL, despite the near-zero aggregate result.
These ratios cannot serve as independent measurements of talent or teamwork.

\section{Conclusions}

The evidence does not establish a robust statistical rule explaining GOAT-player status.
Historical careers show that accumulated output, output rate, and comprehensive contribution can give different assessments.
Ruth's WAR lead illustrates why a narrow statistic cannot settle an all-time comparison; Messi's 2015/16 scoring rank illustrates why one season cannot settle a career.
The recent-season output associations fail multiple-testing correction, favourable results depend on sample choices, and the superiority threshold admits almost half the eligible athletes.
The aggregate ratio comparison is further undermined by an exact identity between its variables.

Three lessons follow.
First, the task and observation period determine opponent exposure; a lineup, a game, and a career are different populations.
Second, relative output measures one contribution, while teamwork cannot be identified from unexplained outcomes alone.
Third, a rule of greatness must be evaluated against independently recognised exceptional players and comparison athletes, rather than inferred from count arithmetic or selected famous examples.

The evidence does not show that team-sport GOATs share no meaningful statistical patterns.
It shows that these measures are insufficient to establish them.
A common assessment could still use sport-specific indicators of peer-relative dominance and sustained contribution, but its value requires clearly defined recognition outcomes, features measured before recognition, and validation beyond the sample used to construct it.
Claims that an effect is negligible also require a defined practical margin and \src{https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550617697177}{equivalence testing}; a large ordinary $p$ value does not establish absence.
